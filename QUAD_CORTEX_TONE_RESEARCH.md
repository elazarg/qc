# Quad Cortex tone automation
Research baseline · 18 November 2026

## Assessment

The most promising approach is to **reamp the same dry performance through real Quad Cortex presets, score the results, and audition a small shortlist**. An LLM can research the guitar part, pick plausible ingredients, and construct legal preset candidates. A script can make and measure repeatable changes. Your comparisons determine which differences matter. That yields a measured preset for each guitar and separates three objectives that can disagree: fidelity to the recording, fit in a backing mix, and feel when playing alone.

The Windows host already sees the Quad Cortex through USB audio, MIDI and HID. Cortex Control 4.1.0 is installed. This confirms enumeration and the installed editor version. Firmware, live MCP read/write, and round-trip audio remain unmeasured on this setup. No changes were made to the unit or its settings.

## Matching approaches

| Approach | Use and expected failure mode | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Curated recipe plus LLM | Convert the research into 3–5 candidates with real QC model IDs, parameter bounds, fallback assets, and track roles. Validate the result against firmware, the device catalog, routing, and CPU. An LLM has little direct evidence about the interaction of pickup level, gain and cabinet without a render. | High value for candidate generation. Benchmark it against retrieval alone. |
| Nearest preset from a rendered library | Reamp the same diagnostic phrases through existing presets. Cache per-phrase features and samples; retrieve several sonic families and include a favorite baseline. A preset name or a single riff can mislead. | Low-cost baseline that improves with every session. |
| Spectral matching and EQ/IR fitting | After picking a plausible drive/amp, compare robust average band spectra on similar notes. Fit a smooth, limited post-EQ first; measure whether a short WAV correction IR improves on parametric EQ. [Fractal Tone Match](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/axe-fx-3/Axe-Fx-III-Tone-Match-Manual.pdf) uses a starting preset and a reference. [Cortex Control](https://neuraldsp.com/cortex-control) imports IRs. Corrective filters can copy an artifact, overreact to differences in notes, or conceal a bad choice of gain stage. | Strong incremental tool for cab/mic and broad EQ. |
| Black-box optimization on the QC | Shortlist discrete amp/capture and cabinet/mic choices. Run a coarse design over drive, gain and cuts. Refine a few continuous controls using coordinate search or a gradient-free optimizer, and spend trials where comparisons disagree. Avoid a joint search over every block and knob. | Most direct route to better hardware-native tones. Measure render rate and repeatability before choosing sophistication. |
| Learned effect recognition and style distance | [Sony CSL AutoFX](https://github.com/SonyCSLParis/AutoFX) estimates effect type/parameters and can seed delay, modulation and related choices. [ST-ITO](https://csteinmetz1.github.io/st-ito/) uses a learned production-style metric and inference-time search over non-differentiable chains. Test both on known QC targets and held-out riffs before letting them decide rankings. | Research branch with a credible path to better retrieval and a reference-dependent objective. |
| Offline DSP or neural surrogate | Use compatible plugins, a VST chain, or [NAM](https://www.tone3000.com/guides/neural-amp-modeler) to propose candidate assets or sweep cheaply. Build a QC-specific surrogate only if on-device evaluation is slow and there is enough measured data. WAV IR and amp capture formats are distinct; validate device transfer via supported plugin transfer, equivalent QC blocks or profiling. | Useful after the baseline yields a bottleneck. |
| Neural Capture of a live source | A POD Go or compatible plugin can act as the live processor under test. Try a stable amp/drive/cab core; recreate gain-dependent gating and time effects separately as needed. V1 trains locally and V2 uses Cortex Cloud per the [manual](https://neuraldsp.com/manual/quad-cortex). Validate across input levels and across both guitars. Neural Capture sends known excitation to a target device, so isolated commercial audio is useful for comparison and an actual processor is useful for profiling. | Practical way to preserve the core of a favorite external tone. |
| Arrange the reference role | Estimate tempo-linked delays, stereo width, and whether a sound comes from separate performances. Build a playable main scene, with a separate mix-oriented version if appropriate. Genuine doubles, layered guitars, and production EQ can dominate a studio impression. | High value for track-specific tones, especially where isolated and in-mix preferences diverge. |

## Measurement protocol

**Two guitars.** Record a short reusable DI set for the Charvel and the Les Paul. Record the actual pickup positions and controls you use, tuning, interface input and gain, and a few soft/medium/hard passages. Include open or ringing chords, palm mutes, single notes and sustained notes. Keep raw DI level and use it consistently: volume match the *wet audition*, while input level remains a deliberate part of the tone and the amp’s response. Repeat one phrase for test–retest variation, and keep one phrase out of tuning. A per-guitar variant may need a pickup choice, input trim, pre-EQ, gain, or cabinet change. Evaluate those in order of complexity by replay and preference; body style and brand alone give weak priors.

**Reference tiers.** For the exact same DI passed through a known QC or POD Go target, align processed signals, allow for fixed device latency, and compare multi-resolution spectra, dynamics and timing on the same musical events. For an isolated commercial performance, use passages with similar notes and articulation; lean on broad spectral shape, transient/body balance, saturation and delay timing. Sample-by-sample distance is meaningful chiefly on aligned, paired audio. For a mix or separator output, downweight fine spectra and stereo artifacts and audition the candidate against the backing. Log the target role, reference quality and rough weights.

**Listen and validate.** Use matched perceived volume and a consistent listening chain. Compare attack, decay, note separation and noise between phrases, plus low-end buildup, upper-mid emphasis, fizz, modulation and delay/reverb where relevant. Evaluate live responsiveness with guitar volume changes and pickup changes. Preserve natural dynamic differences in measurement; apply audition gain after render. Keep repeat renders and cache inputs, preset state, output/monitoring path, firmware, dependency IDs and objective scores. A final preset can include per-song scenes for rhythm, lead and clean sections, with consistent level and documented pickup choices.

## First experiment design

1. Confirm actual CorOS version and read the current grid. Verify pitch, routing and input gain with a bypass reamp and a known amp/cab chain. Check that capture IDs, scene settings and one deliberate parameter change are reflected in the recording. Measure time per render and run-to-run variance.
2. Render one QC favorite and one POD Go favorite if accessible using the same DI. Use a known held-out QC tone as the control case and choose one commercial song section with clear guitar as the first realistic case.
3. Compare the current favorite, the nearest already-rendered preset, an agent-researched recipe, that recipe with fitted post-EQ, and a small hardware search over amp/cab/drive. Compare on both guitars with the same protocol. Retain a held-out phrase and a level-matched blind shortlist to catch feature overfitting.
4. Report render cost, preference against the baseline, fidelity to reference, and portability across the two guitars. Use those results to decide whether the next investment belongs in reference collection, Windows audio automation, learned metrics, or expanding the preset library.

The first milestone is one playable, repeatable comparison on each guitar. A reusable recipe needs the reference and timestamp, guitar and pickup, QC model/asset IDs, settings and scenes, gain/routing assumptions, and a short record of what won the comparison.
