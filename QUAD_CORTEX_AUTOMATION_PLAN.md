# Quad Cortex tone automation plan

18 November 2026 · Charvel and Les Paul · Windows

The first useful system is a repeatable Quad Cortex audition. Record a short clean performance from each guitar, replay it through candidate tones, and audition the best few at matched loudness. Use an agent to research the guitar part, find plausible starting points, and interpret feedback. Use local code for device control, audio routing, measurement, and replay. Save the chosen settings with the song section, guitar and pickup so they can be recalled later.

This plan builds on the [prior research](QUAD_CORTEX_TONE_RESEARCH.md). The prior report’s experiment steps are proposals. The observations below reflect checks on this machine.

## Where the tones come from

| Source | How to turn it into a useful starting point |
|---|---|
| **Your QC presets** | Start with favorites and familiar scenes. Read preset state, routing, and capture, IR and plugin dependencies. Keep the original snapshot and render a small library indexed by the sound. Device Presets in CorOS 4.1 can also preserve an individual amp, drive or effect setting for reuse. [Neural DSP release notes](https://neuraldsp.com/quad-cortex-updates/coros-and-cortex-control-4-1-0-are-now-available) |
| **Your POD Go archive** | Recover model IDs, signal order, cab/mic or IR references, and per-snapshot gain, bypass and delay settings. Translate by amp/cab family and block function. Verify knob scales by ear and measurement. A POD Go recording of the same DI is a stronger target than settings alone. If the amp/drive/cab core matters most, test Neural Capture of the external chain and recreate modulation, delay and reverb as QC blocks. [POD Go manuals](https://line6.com/support/manuals/podgo/) · [QC Neural Capture and IRs](https://neuraldsp.com/manual/quad-cortex) |
| **Song and recording** | Specify release/version, timestamp, target guitar and its role. Search session interviews, rig rundowns and isolated tracks. Label hypotheses by evidence, especially where recording and touring rigs differ. In a dense mix, test a guitar-capable separator and check its output against the original for bleed and missing attacks. Use a mix-based audition when guitars are layered or doubled. [Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs) |
| **Cortex Cloud, factory tones and plugins** | Try a few plausible recipes, captures and cabs, then audition with the same DI. Verify dependencies and whether the capture includes a cab. The live grid establishes that Plini devices are present on the QC. Installed John Mayer and Tim Henson plugin folders are leads to verify for licensing and versions. Compatible plugins can supply extra presets or fast offline renders. [Cortex Cloud Web](https://neuraldsp.com/quad-cortex-updates/cortex-cloud-web-is-now-available) · [Plugin compatibility release](https://neuraldsp.com/quad-cortex-updates/coros-and-cortex-control-4-1-0-are-now-available) |
| **Spotify and feedback** | Use play counts, skips, repeats, playlists and eras from an account export to prioritize songs. Keep the import to relevant track fields. Use locally available reference audio for DSP. Ask for brief comparisons such as “A has better pick attack” and record whether the comparison was solo or against a backing track. Playback history identifies music to investigate. Auditions identify which tones to keep. [Spotify data export](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/understanding-your-data/) · [Spotify Developer Policy](https://developer.spotify.com/policy) |

## How to search and rank tones

| Approach | First use | How to judge it |
|---|---|---|
| Rendered preset retrieval | Compare a small library of favorites and contrasting clean, crunch and lead presets. Include the current favorite as a baseline. | Time to a playable shortlist and how often a retrieved tone beats the baseline. |
| Research plus agent recipes | Generate a few legal candidates from available device IDs. For example, vary the amp family, cab and drive while preserving the same guitar role. | Candidate validity, quality of source attribution, and comparison against retrieval using the same DI. |
| Cabinet, microphone and post-EQ fitting | Once gain and feel are close, fit modest EQ adjustments from comparable phrases. Try available IRs or a smooth correction filter. | Check held-out phrases. A narrow fit can follow different notes, room sound or separation artifacts. [Fractal Tone Match manual](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/axe-fx-3/Axe-Fx-III-Tone-Match-Manual.pdf) |
| Bounded hardware search | Shortlist discrete amp/cab choices, then sweep gain, drive and cuts over a small range. Refine the few parameters that measurably or audibly matter. Cache repeats. | Compare quality per minute and rank consistency across repeat renders. Allow for effect tails and time-varying modulation. |
| Plugin or DSP trials | Try alternatives offline when real-time reamps become a bottleneck. Render finalists on the QC, including scenes and relevant output routing. | Count how often an offline ranking survives hardware playback. |
| Effect identification and learned distance | Trial AutoFX as an effect hypothesis generator and ST-ITO as an additional ranking signal for cross-performance comparisons. | Hold out riffs and compare to simple features and blind preferences. [AutoFX](https://github.com/SonyCSLParis/AutoFX) · [ST-ITO](https://csteinmetz1.github.io/st-ito/) |
| Preference learning | Start by retrieving prior choices with similar guitar, role and monitoring context. Train a pairwise ranker once there are enough varied comparisons. | Does it predict future choices better than measured similarity or a favorite-preset baseline? |

Use different evidence for different targets. Same-DI QC or POD Go renders can be time-aligned and compared on the same notes. Commercial references are better suited to broad spectral balance, saturation, attack and time effects, especially when the playing differs. Keep recording fidelity, fit in a backing mix and live feel as separate audition criteria. Use perceived-loudness matching on playback and preserve raw capture levels for measurement. Log clipping, noise, latency, tails and repeatability before trusting aggregate scores.

## System for playing and talking

1. **Keep live monitoring on the Quad Cortex.** Your guitar goes directly through the QC to your normal monitoring chain. A Windows service handles commands and recording in parallel. Plugin hosting and speech processing stay outside the live signal path.
2. **Start with a saved DAW session, then automate the measured bottleneck.** Studio Pro 8 can route ASIO channels and run macros, so use it for the first manual round trip. Under default QC routing, record guitar from DAW input 1 and stereo wet audio from inputs 3/4. Send the dry track to DAW output 5 and choose USB Input 5 on the test grid to reamp. Check the Dry/Wet Swap setting and keep the wet return out of the reamp send. Audacity can inspect and trim files. If unattended recording becomes the bottleneck, use REAPER with ReaScript or a dedicated ASIO host. [Neural DSP USB workflow](https://neuraldsp.com/getting-started/using-quad-cortex-as-an-audio-interface) · [Studio Pro routing](https://fenderstudiopromanual.fender.com/en/Content/Setup_Topics/Audio_Device_Input_Output.htm) · [Studio Pro macros](https://fenderstudiopromanual.fender.com/en/Content/Editing_Topics/Macro_Toolbar.htm) · [Audacity ASIO support](https://manual.audacityteam.org/man/asio_audio_interface.html) · [ReaScript](https://www.reaper.fm/sdk/reascript/reascripthelp)
3. **Give the agent a few reversible actions.** Inspect the current preset, select a guitar/profile, create a working candidate, change a bounded parameter, record a clip, play A/B, undo, and save a named winner. Keep one owner of the QC session and validate routing, CPU, model availability, and asset dependencies. Read back edits. Run any bulk search on a dedicated test preset at a conservative listening level. Keep global settings out of the candidate recipe.
4. **Use talk and play as alternating turns.** A first voice version can use a separate mic and push-to-talk, with the guitar on its own DI input. For example, “Les Paul bridge. Save this riff. Give me two versions with tighter low end.” Replay the captured riff, present A/B, then take “B, with less gain” as a bounded edit. A small local control panel can expose record, current preset/scene, A/B, undo and save. The same controls support text and keyboard use. Add continuous conversation only after the push-to-talk workflow is reliable, and use headphones or appropriate echo cancellation for spoken feedback.
5. **Store recipes with the evidence needed to replay them.** At minimum keep the song/version/timestamp and role, guitar/pickup and DI level, monitoring setup, exact routing and blocks, scenes, capture and IR IDs, firmware, render clips, and comparison notes. Keep source files locally and record provenance for each song-specific claim.

## First session and success criteria

1. Pick one section with an audible guitar, one existing QC tone, and one POD Go candidate if relevant. Plini is an immediately available QC anchor. The best POD Go anchor is whichever tone you remember enjoying.
2. Record 15–30 seconds each from the Charvel and Les Paul with the pickup positions and control settings you actually use. Include palm mutes, chords, and lightly and firmly picked notes. Write down pickup position, tuning and monitor path. Retain one phrase for later validation.
3. Use a bypass pass to check the routing, latency and level. Compare a live wet take with the reamped wet take from its own DI. Reamp twice, checking alignment, peaks and noise, then verify that one controlled tweak yields the expected audible change.
4. Compare the QC anchor, a retrieved or researched preset, and a refined version. Swap the same recipe between the two guitars first. If the ranking changes, try input trim, pre-EQ or gain as a small per-guitar adjustment, then test a different cab/amp if required. Retain a separate variant for each guitar when it wins the comparison.
5. Deliver recallable scenes, level-matched WAVs and a short record of what won solo and in the backing track. The milestone is complete when each guitar has a playable result that can be recalled and re-recorded consistently. A successful benchmark also tells us whether the next investment should be more source tones, faster recording, better matching, or voice interaction.
