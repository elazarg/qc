# Quad Cortex capabilities for Tone Lab

## Inventory from the connected Quad Cortex

The full-size unit reported **CorOS 4.1.1**. The stock editor identified itself as Cortex Control 4.1.0. A read-only directory scan verified the preset address and scene before and after the scan. The catalog is available locally at `tone_sessions/qc-library.json` for browsing during a Cortex Control session. It contains names, identifiers, and folder membership, with audio files and credentials excluded.

| What was observed | What the Lab can do with it |
| --- | --- |
| **165 named working presets** across seven existing user setlists, excluding two existing Lab fixtures. There were also 59 preset entries under Downloads and extensive factory and plugin folders. | Reuse the named on-device preset and scene as the durable sound. Treat Downloads and plugin folders as distinct namespaces. The mere presence of a plugin's preset folder does not establish the required license. |
| **Tone Lab**, a 256-slot user setlist created through Cortex Control. **Europa Bloom** was saved in **1A** and verified after recall. The unit returned to **Vai for The Love God**, scene A. | Save new tones into vacant slots here. A recorded phrase can change independently. A saved slot, preset name, scene, and verified content fingerprint identify the tone. |

Ninety-five bear the `YA MES 212 V30` family label, spanning mic positions and blends. `My IRs` contains links to these same assets. `Selected IRs` was empty. A factory IR row and an imported `CIR_` binding take different paths. Filenames are useful search hints and still need an audio audition. |

## How Quad Cortex is designed to remember sounds

| Native mechanism | Useful division of work |
| --- | --- |
| **Preset and setlist.** A preset stores the chain, routing, parameters, assignments, and scenes. Banks on this model have eight positions. | Keep a tone as a native preset. Store the inventory address and an expected name or fingerprint locally, with revision history. Render the next phrase through that preset. Send a genuinely new candidate to a vacant Tone Lab slot. |
| **Scenes.** Eight per preset. A Scene recalls multiple assigned parameters and bypass states. The active scene at save can become the default. | Use named scenes for rehearsable differences within one chain, such as rhythm/lead or a roomier version. A Lab preference is reusable only when its scene is identified. Use separate presets when the amp, effects topology, or capture and IR dependencies change. |
| **Stomp, Hybrid, and Gig View.** Stomp controls can bind several blocks. Hybrid mixes different footswitch purposes. Gig View surfaces the assignments. | Keep performance controls on the hardware. A Lab-generated preset can be auditioned and then assigned actual performance functions in Cortex Control. |
| **Virtual Device Presets.** CorOS 4.1 provides up to 32 saved parameter configurations per eligible device, I/O Settings, and Global EQ. Device settings may be used as defaults. | Retain a working amp setting or I/O profile without duplicating the whole rig. Associate guitar and monitoring profiles with these separately from the guitar tone. Paired A/B should keep the I/O and Global EQ profile constant. |
| **Grid.** Four rows of eight positions. Splitters and mixers support serial and parallel processing, level and pan, crossover, and phase. The available processor budget still constrains what fits. | Use complementary parallel branches for tests such as a little clean amp under a driven amp. Compare it with a single-chain baseline at the same final loudness. Use a dedicated output control for level, and inspect CPU before proposing a live performance rig. Keep the routing editor in Cortex Control. |
| **Audio and Looper X.** USB is 48 kHz and offers eight channels in each direction. Dry/wet assignments and headphone USB source are configurable. The Looper X can capture the grid or selected inputs/outputs, with foot controls, threshold, pre-roll, and one-shot playback. | Use the USB driver to archive a reusable dry performance. Explore an input looper for local hardware audition while keeping the desktop recording as durable evidence. Test whether its audio persists and aligns across the specific preset and routing changes before using it as the default. |
| **MIDI and expression.** Setlist selection and preset recall use Bank Select and Program Change. CC 43 selects scenes, CC 1 and 2 are expression positions, and Looper X has dedicated controls. QC can emit configured MIDI on preset load and qualifying footswitch events. | Trial a mapped pedal for hands-free A/B and record controls. MIDI is a native performance surface with bounded fast actions. It does not supply an authoritative feed of all external grid edits or arbitrary individual parameters. |
| **Cortex Control.** Native setlist creation, block editing, browsing, backups, drag-and-drop IR import, and supported plugin preset transfer. | Use it for setup, asset import, and fine edits. Tone Lab interprets an intent, identifies a safe next sound, manages audition evidence, and remembers what the player chose. |

These roles follow the manufacturer’s descriptions of the Directory, Grid, Device Presets, MIDI, audio I/O, and editor. ### Models, captures, cabinets, and dependencies

- The [official Quad Cortex device list](https://neuraldsp.com/device-list) documents built-in models, the real gear some were based on, and required plugin licenses. The installed automation catalog is a bundled `ModelRepo.xml` snapshot that maps model IDs and parameter schemas. That snapshot and the on-unit Directory answer different questions. Licensing and current device readback remain decisive.
- A capture may represent a pedal, a head, a mic'd combo, a cabinet, or an entire rig. V1 training can run on the unit. V2 training uses Cortex Control and Cortex Cloud. The two are distinct block engines. Avoid adding another cab stage unless a capture is known to be direct or unless a comparison deliberately tests the composite. [Neural Capture V2](https://neuraldsp.com/news/introducing-neural-capture-version-2)
- Imported IRs are discrete cabinet responses. Variation within this user's abundant Mesa 2×12 family offers a controlled search. Hold amp, drive, playing, and levels fixed, change only the response, and audition separately from an amp or saturation search.
- Capture and IR choices must use stable keys. Display and folder names can repeat. Capture folders commonly contain links with a key and an empty name. On device readback, the capture key and name are stored together and the matching generic model ID identifies V1 or V2. A custom IR binding carries a separate `CIR_` path and name. [Pinned qc-mcp directory notes](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/lexasoft123/qc-mcp/f634ecd3dcf426090b90bc4cc176e4b500494b9e/docs/DIRECTORY.md)

### Device access and reliability

An explicit hardware action acquires a short exclusive handoff. Idle library browsing uses the last scan. The installed transport has verified primitives for reading the Directory, recalling existing presets, and saving the current grid to a position. Its public API lacks verified creation of new setlists. The initial setlist was therefore created through the stock UI. The device remained on its original preset and scene. Keep the writer on verified empty positions, require a complete slot table, and verify the saved preset after recall. If the player has unsaved edits, let them resolve those on the native hardware before a switching operation.

Cortex Control and Tone Lab should exchange control at clear points while the listening session stays open. Earlier shared-USB experiments on this machine interrupted Cortex Control. The upstream shared-session notes describe tests that succeeded elsewhere and identify multi-report traffic hazards. Use standard MIDI for experiments that need supported instant actions while the editor runs. Keep writes, load verification, and reconciliation separate until tested together. ## Tone spaces to explore on this device

| Question the player can ask | Experiment that can make the answer useful |
| --- | --- |
| “Where's the sound between too pointy and too soft?” | An **attack × focus** surface. Map horizontal position to cab/IR and upper-mid emphasis. Map vertical position to dynamics and pick transient retention. Hold fixed loudness and the amp. First characterize a few corners with the same phrase, then adjust one control at a time by ear. |
| “Can I make this gain sound clearer without losing sustain?” | A **saturation × articulation** surface. Compare input drive, pre-EQ, and a clean/drive blend with output compensation. Add a repeatable short-pick passage and a sustained note. Report separately which change helped each. |
| “What can my IRs give me?” | An **IR neighborhood** based on measured spectral envelope, phase, and early response, grouped by confirmed asset family. Compare a familiar mix with one contrasting mic and one off-axis position. Names such as Mix 04 identify exact files but need measurement before placement. |
| “I like the sound from a record, but I want it to play better.” | Keep the familiar preset, audition a few constrained candidates on the same phrase, then test the favorite live for touch. Keep a short trail of preferred and rejected moves. Combine signal evidence with preference feedback and decide by ear how strongly to pursue matching the recording's dynamics or ambience. |
| “Make the space enjoyable alone and firm with the band.” | An **ambience × mix reach** comparison. Lock the amp and cabinet, move delay, reverb, placement, or room EQ. Listen in both full-range output and mono, with the same contextual level, plus a backing mix when available. |
| “Find the missing dimension.” | An experimental reachable-regions view that compares qualitatively different structures first, then interpolates compatible parameters only. Use a continuous surface for a single topology and stepped landmarks for changing capture, cabinet, or series/parallel routing. Label the evidence behind each location. |

Fixed-performance reamping exposes small differences. Live playing catches feel, articulation changes, response to the player's hands, and muscle memory. Tone Lab's Guitar and Phrase transport uses one working grid and shape for both inputs. A USB-only test switched a recall of Europa Bloom from Guitar to Phrase, delivered the saved DI, then restored Guitar and finally the starting preset. A separate test bound **YA MES 212 V30 Mix 01** by its device key and returned valid level-matched A/B audio. These checks confirm routing and asset deployment. Artistic suitability and a useful map of the imported IRs still need listening.
