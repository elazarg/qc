# Tone Lab product direction

## What the guitarist should be able to ask

The job is to find a sound worth playing with the guitar in hand, then get back to playing. Start with an artist, a recording, a favorite preset, or a change the player can describe. Keep one conversation open through the first audition, further adjustments, and saving the result. Let the player stay in Live while the Lab handles research, routing, and candidate comparisons.

| A guitarist might say | What Tone Lab should do |
|---|---|
| “Make me a Lenny Kravitz preset.” | Pick a named starting point, disclose the assumption, and draft an editable Quad Cortex chain for the selected guitar. “The Are You Gonna Go My Way riff” is a useful first choice. Kravitz recalls using a Gibson Skylark and a Les Paul for that song, and a different set of amps for *Let Love Rule*. The interview supports a starting hypothesis. The resulting patch still needs an audition. [Kravitz interview](https://www.guitarworld.com/magazine/dear-guitar-hero-lenny-kravitz-talks-vintage-gear-let-love-rule-and-new-album-strut) |
| “Make this sound like the guitar at 0:42.” | Identify the version, section, and guitar part. Use the best available reference and say when it is a full mix. Render the same saved dry take through a few candidates on the QC and let the player hear the top choices at comparable volume. |
| “Use my old preset for the comparison.” | Let a named device preset serve as the reference or the starting chain. For a sound comparison, replay the same dry take through that preset and keep its processed result. Retain the original and return to the active Lab patch. Line 6 also documents exporting individual POD Go presets as `.pgp` files, which can provide a recipe alongside any reference audio available from the unit. [POD Go Edit pilot’s guide](https://line6.com/data/6/0a00050e077d66676e26ed5da/application/pdf/POD%2520Go%2520Edit%2520-%2520Pilot%27s%2520Guide%2520v2.0%2520-%2520English%2520.pdf) |
| “More jazz, less bite, keep the attack.” | Make changes within the current sound through bounded controls. Let a knob adjust several parameters together and let a short comment keep part of the tone steady. Offer a one-click comparison and undo. |
| “I’m on the Charvel now. Make these work at rehearsal.” | Recall the guitar and pickup profile. Check input headroom first, keep final-volume adjustments downstream of the drive, and measure rhythm and lead levels on representative playing. Preview room or front-of-house EQ as an output choice. Keep an intentional solo boost. |

The dry take belongs to the **performance**, the wet reference defines the **target**, and each processed take is a **candidate**. Feedback belongs to the candidate and the listening context. That structure lets someone swap a reference, judge several tones, or return tomorrow without re-recording or explaining the whole session again. A useful audition needs a complete take, dependable playback, and a consistent monitoring level. Measurements can rank candidates, and the player selects which to keep.

## What already exists

- **Positive Grid REACTOR** is the closest shipped comparison I found. Its Creator Hub takes text, speech, photos, or a song clip, and can refine a current tone through chat. Its song workflow presents up to four variations. Generated cards preview on the amp, and Positive Grid says thumbs-up/down feedback informs future suggestions. [Creator Hub guide](https://help.positivegrid.com/hc/en-us/articles/45696624704525-Creator-Hub)
- **Fractal Axe-Fx III** applies spectral matching between a local starting patch and a recording or live reference. Fractal’s editors also give preset-level meters and adjustment controls. These are precedents for a comparison with a real signal and an assisted leveling pass. [Fractal Tone Match](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/axe-fx-3/Axe-Fx-III-Tone-Match-Manual.pdf) · [Fractal preset leveling](https://www.fractalaudio.com/downloads/manuals/FM9/FM9-Owners-Manual.pdf)
- **IK Multimedia TONEX, Neural Capture V2, and Line 6 Helix Stadium Proxy** model a physical source with a capture workflow. This is useful when the original amp or processor is available. Stadium provides an original/clone audition and its desktop app manages preset libraries. Its assignable X/Y controller and external triggers are precedents for adjusting several sound parameters from a single gesture. [TONEX](https://www.ikmultimedia.com/products/tonexecosystem/) · [Neural Capture V2](https://neuraldsp.com/news/introducing-neural-capture-version-2) · [Stadium cloning](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/live/clones) · [Stadium presets](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/live/presets) · [Stadium controllers](https://manuals.line6.com/en/helix-stadium/rev-d-v1.3/bypass-and-controller-assign)
- **Fender Tone Master Pro** organizes tones around songs and setlists, offers named Global EQ settings, and can put its looper before or after the instrument chain. **Quad Cortex** already supports simultaneous dry and wet USB capture, USB reamping, separate I/O and Global EQ presets, and local backups through Cortex Control. These are useful building blocks for auditioning a fixed take and adapting a saved tone to a venue. [Fender release notes](https://support.fender.com/hc/en-us/articles/46768129660315-Fender-Tone-Master-Pro-Firmware-and-Pro-Control-App-Updates) · [Neural DSP audio interface guide](https://neuraldsp.com/getting-started/using-quad-cortex-as-an-audio-interface) · [Quad Cortex manual](https://neuraldsp.com/manual/quad-cortex) · [Cortex Control](https://neuraldsp.com/cortex-control)

## Where Tone Lab can go further

One Neural DSP forum request pairs reference-audio preset generation with a dry loop for hands-free tweaking and A/B. Other players ask for instrument profiles independent of song presets and scene-aware leveling. The closest opportunity for Tone Lab is bringing those together on the user’s Quad Cortex, with their own guitar, preset library, saved performances, and listening preferences. Those threads document individual requests. [Reference audio and DI loop](https://unity.neuraldsp.com/t/ai-audio-to-tone-matching-built-in-di-virtual-input-for-tone-tweaking/23983) · [Instrument profiles](https://unity.neuraldsp.com/t/global-instrument-profiles-for-live-input-calibration/23580) · [Preset and scene leveling](https://unity.neuraldsp.com/t/normalize-patch-volume/16157)

Prioritize the short loop from **ask → hear → compare → keep**. Once that loop works reliably, saved comparisons can improve which candidates the Lab offers for each guitar. Optional guitar gestures can then become another input. Begin with a discrete gesture learned per instrument, such as a switch movement that returns to its starting position, and test it against normal playing. Continuous tone-knob control is a separate experiment because the knob itself changes the guitar’s signal and feel. Keep both experiments easy to disarm during an audition or performance.
